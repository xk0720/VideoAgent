# TASK D — Dialogue + Audio-Visual Sync Research (2025–2026)

## 1. Native-audio generation (the zero-extra-cost path)

**Seedance 2.0 `generate_audio` — the strongest option for Maestro, and it's free.** WaveSpeed's seedance-2.0 endpoints (the ones Maestro already calls) expose `generate_audio` as an optional boolean on image-to-video (also text-to-video and video-extend), and audio incurs **no price delta** — pricing scales only with size/length/count/references ([WaveSpeed i2v model page](https://wavespeed.ai/models/bytedance/seedance-2.0/image-to-video); base $0.60 per 480p/5s run, 720p 2x, 1080p 5x, 4K 10x; [WaveSpeed Seedance 2 API overview](https://wavespeed.ai/seedance-2-api) says "native audio across all tiers"). Multiple guides confirm "audio is part of the unified pipeline, not a separate billable step" — muting doesn't reduce cost ([Cutout.pro audio guide](https://www.cutout.pro/learn/blog-seedance-2-0-audio-guide/); [Kensa guide](https://www.kensa.cc/blog/seedance-2-0-audio-generation-guide): "completely free—no credit premium").

**What it produces:** four layers in one pass — lip-synced dialogue (TTS converted to phoneme-level audio that *conditions mouth movement*), synchronized SFX (impacts, mechanical, nature, human sounds), ambient soundscapes, and style-guided music ([Kensa](https://www.kensa.cc/blog/seedance-2-0-audio-generation-guide)). **Dialogue prompting = quoted lines in the prompt**: `character says "Welcome to our spring collection."`; specify language explicitly for non-English; **5–10 words per line is the sweet spot**; mix control via explicit prompt text like "Dialogue clean and prominent, music low, ambient subtle" ([Cutout.pro](https://www.cutout.pro/learn/blog-seedance-2-0-audio-guide/)). Lip-sync quality: English/Mandarin excellent, Japanese/Korean/Spanish good, French/German fair; degrades on monologues >15s and **wide shots (low face resolution) produce imprecise lips** — best with medium close-up, locked camera, front-facing ([Kensa](https://www.kensa.cc/blog/seedance-2-0-audio-generation-guide), [Cutout.pro](https://www.cutout.pro/learn/blog-seedance-2-0-audio-guide/)). Reviewers report Seedance is "a frame or two tighter than Veo in lip-sync precision," 8+ languages ([search summary across seedance guides](https://www.easemate.ai/seedance-2-0-ai-video-generator)).

**Competitors:** [Veo 3.1](https://modelslab.com/blog/api/veo-3-1-vs-kling-3-sora-2-ai-video-api-cost-2026) has best-regarded native dialogue but costs $0.40/s standard ($0.15/s fast) — ~8–30x Seedance. [Sora 2](https://costgoat.com/pricing/sora) ($0.10/s; Pro $0.30–0.70/s) has native dialogue but **OpenAI's Videos API/sora-2/sora-2-pro are scheduled for removal Sept 24, 2026** (deprecation notice Mar 24, 2026) — dead end. [Kling 3.0](https://modelslab.com/blog/api/veo-3-1-vs-kling-3-sora-2-ai-video-api-cost-2026) Standard ~$0.084/s with audio; Kling 3.0 Omni demonstrated multi-character per-mouth phoneme sync, but Kling outputs are reported to have "floating mouth" artifacts vs. competitors ([lipsync.com HeyGen-vs-Kling](https://lipsync.com/compare/heygen-vs-kling)). **Conclusion: nothing beats Seedance 2.0 native audio on cost for Maestro since it's the incumbent model at $0 delta.**

## 2. Post-hoc lip-sync tools — and the animal-character question

**Critical finding: for animal characters (cats), the mainstream post-hoc lip-sync stack does NOT work.**

- **LatentSync / MuseTalk / Wav2Lip / SadTalker**: all depend on human face detection (crop ROI to 256×256); research on animated characters reports LatentSync produces incoherent lips and MuseTalk breaks identity preservation on stylized faces ([SayAnything paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.11515v1), [MuseTalk paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.10122v2)). Not viable for cats.
- **sync.so (lipsync-2, lipsync-2-pro, sync-3)**: official docs state plainly: *"You can lipsync human-like faces, but our models don't currently support animals or non-humanoid characters"* ([sync.so docs](https://sync.so/docs/models/lipsync)) — despite a marketing page claiming "as long as a mouth-like structure is identified, Sync can animate it" ([ai.sync.so](https://ai.sync.so/lip-sync-non-human-characters)). Treat docs as authoritative; marketing claim unverified. WaveSpeed hosts [lipsync-2-pro at $0.08/s of audio](https://wavespeed.ai/models/sync/lipsync-2-pro) ("works on real humans, 2D/3D animation, synthetic avatars" — humanoid only).
- **ByteDance LipSync on WaveSpeed** ([audio-to-video](https://wavespeed.ai/models/bytedance/lipsync/audio-to-video), $0.15/5s audio, ≤600s): human-face oriented, no non-human claims.
- **The one that claims animals: ByteDance OmniHuman 1.5** — audio-driven avatar generation from a single image, explicitly "works with animals, cartoon characters, and stylized illustrations… even pets" ([BytePlus OmniHuman](https://www.byteplus.com/en/product/OmniHuman), [Layer model page](https://www.layer.ai/models/bytedance--omnihuman)); on WaveSpeed at [$0.12/s (1.0) / ~$0.16/s (1.5)](https://wavespeed.ai/docs/docs-api/bytedance/bytedance-avatar-omni-human). **Caveat: it's image+audio→video (it generates the shot), not video→video retrofit** — so it replaces shot generation for dialogue close-ups rather than patching an existing Seedance shot, breaking Maestro's tail-conditioning continuity. [InfiniteTalk video-to-video](https://wavespeed.ai/models/wavespeed-ai/infinitetalk/video-to-video) (WaveSpeed, $0.15–0.30/5s) IS video-to-video dubbing, but no documented animal support.
- **HeyGen**: $0.10/s but $100/month API minimum, slowest tested ([lipsync.com](https://lipsync.com/compare/heygen-vs-kling)); avatar/human-focused. **Kling's lip-sync feature**: native in Kling 2.6/3.0 generation, but requires Kling-generated video and has floating-mouth artifacts ([aivideobootcamp](https://aivideobootcamp.com/blog/kling-ai-complete-guide-pricing-features-prompts-tips/)).

## 3. TTS for character voices

Per-1M-character pricing ([buildmvpfast roundup](https://www.buildmvpfast.com/api-costs/ai-voice), [texttolab](https://texttolab.com/blog/openai-tts-pricing)): **OpenAI tts-1 $15 / tts-1-hd $30 / gpt-4o-mini-tts ≈$0.015 per audio-minute**; **ElevenLabs $103–206** (premium, best quality/controllability); Hume Octave 2 $7.60; Inworld TTS-1.5 $10; **Qwen3-TTS (open-weight, Jan 2026) ~$13/1M hosted or free self-hosted**, Qwen-Audio-3.0-TTS $27.59/1M ([digitalapplied](https://www.digitalapplied.com/blog/qwen-audio-3-0-tts-price-war-voice-stack-2026)); **edge-tts free** (unofficial Microsoft Edge endpoint wrapper, no API key — ToS/stability risk, no SLA) ([SourceForge mirror](https://sourceforge.net/projects/edge-tts.mirror/)); Piper = free/local/CPU. Voice consistency across shots is trivial with TTS (fixed voice ID per character) — this is TTS's structural advantage over native audio. Dialogue volume for a narrative film is tiny (a whole film's dialogue < 10K chars → **under $0.25 even on OpenAI**), so TTS cost is negligible; the expensive part is lip-sync, not TTS.

## 4. Architecture question + audio continuity across extend chains

Industry pattern in 2026 is **hybrid, tilting native**: native audio is the default for speed and lip-sync authenticity ("silent AI video is considered obsolete" — [Renoise state-of-AI-video](https://renoise.ai/blog/state-of-ai-video-2026)), while "for clean, high-control results, many professional creators still generate silent video, then layer voice and sound in post"; TTS overdub wins on voice fidelity/brand control ([aimagicx guide](https://www.aimagicx.com/blog/ai-video-native-audio-generation-guide-2026)).

**Continuity evidence — the decisive part for Maestro:**
- WaveSpeed's seedance-2.0 **video-extend keeps `generate_audio`, preserves the input clip's audio, and generates native audio for the new segment** ([WaveSpeed video-extend page](https://wavespeed.ai/models/bytedance/seedance-2.0/video-extend): "Native audio generation is included for the new segment; the original video's audio is preserved"). Note extend billing is much higher per second ($0.24/s at 720p) than i2v.
- **But Seedance has NO voice cloning: "Consistent character voice across multiple clips: No"** — audio references are timing guides only, capped ~15s, and the model synthesizes a new voice each generation ([seedance2pro.io voice-reference analysis](https://seedance2pro.io/blog/does-seedance-2-accept-voice-reference)). So a character speaking in shot 3 and shot 7 (separate i2v generations conditioned on a tail *frame*) will likely get **different voices** — the #1 risk of pure native audio for Maestro. First-frame conditioning carries zero audio context.
- Veo 3.1's extend explicitly seeds from the last second including audio for "native audio continuation" up to 148s ([apiyi guide](https://help.apiyi.com/en/veo-3-1-video-extend-guide-en.html), [RunComfy](https://www.runcomfy.com/models/google-deepmind/veo-3-1/extend-video)) — evidence the extend-chain (not frame-chain) is the industry mechanism for audio continuity.
- Community workflow consensus for narrative: **"keep Seedance's SFX and BGM, mute the AI dialogue, drop in recorded/TTS voiceover"** ([seedance2pro.io](https://seedance2pro.io/blog/does-seedance-2-accept-voice-reference)) — i.e., native audio for sound bed, external audio for voices when consistency matters.

**Implication for Maestro (cat characters):** enable `generate_audio` (free) for ambience/SFX/music bed; for dialogue, either (a) accept native quoted-dialogue lip-sync per shot and tolerate cross-shot voice drift, (b) route dialogue shots through Maestro's existing video-extend path (audio-preserving) so the voice context carries, or (c) mute native dialogue and overdub TTS **without** lip-sync retrofit — because no hosted video-to-video lip-sync tool documents animal support.

## 5. SFX/ambience (brief)

Native Seedance SFX/ambience is free and frame-accurate ("glass breaking on frame 47 sounds on frame 47" — [ugccopilot](https://ugccopilot.ai/blog/seedance-2-native-audio-generation-guide/)); post options: [Freesound](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freesound) (free, CC), ElevenLabs SFX (credit-billed, plans from free/10K credits to $6–99/mo — [puter breakdown](https://developer.puter.com/tutorials/elevenlabs-api-pricing/)). BGM post options: Google **Lyria 2 $0.06/30s** on Vertex ([cometapi](https://www.cometapi.com/suno-4-5-vs-googles-lyria-2/)), Suno via unofficial APIs $0.014–0.111/song ([sunor](https://sunor.cc/blog/suno-api-pricing-2026)). Native wins on sync; post wins on control/looping a consistent score across the whole film (native music would change per shot — concat would produce jarring music cuts, so **film-level BGM should be post** regardless).

## Honest unknowns needing playground verification

1. **Voice drift magnitude**: does the same described cat character get a recognizably different voice across two separate seedance-2.0 i2v generations? (Docs imply yes; no measurements found.)
2. **Whether seedance native dialogue lip-syncs a CAT's mouth at all** — all lip-sync-quality reports are about human faces; no source tested quoted dialogue on animal characters in Seedance 2.0.
3. **Extend-chain audio in practice**: does WaveSpeed video-extend actually continue a mid-sentence voice coherently, and does music continue or restart at the boundary?
4. **sync.so animal claim**: docs vs. marketing contradiction ([docs](https://sync.so/docs/models/lipsync) vs [ai.sync.so](https://ai.sync.so/lip-sync-non-human-characters)) — one $0.08/s test on a cat clip would settle it.
5. **OmniHuman 1.5 on a stylized cat image**: claimed pet support needs a real test, plus whether its output can visually match Seedance shots well enough to intercut.
6. Whether `generate_audio` output is affected by Maestro's ffmpeg concat pipeline (streams must be concat-compatible; current pipeline assumes video-only).
